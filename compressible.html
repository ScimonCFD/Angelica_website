Compressible Solver

Gas networks where density follows pressure.

The hydraulic solver provides the pressure field and mass flow rates. The compressible solver wraps it in an outer loop that re-evaluates density from an equation of state at each updated pressure field, iterating until the density distribution converges.

Applicability

Built for single-phase gas networks.

The compressible solver extends the isothermal hydraulic solver for cases where the gas density varies with pressure along the network. It is the right choice for natural gas distribution, compressed air, and any single-component gas pipeline where the pressure ratio is large enough that an incompressible assumption would introduce significant error.

Valid for

Steady-state flow of single-phase gases modelled by an equation of state — currently ideal gas ($\rho = PM/RT$). Isothermal assumption: temperature is uniform and constant along the network.

Not valid for

Two-phase flow or conditions near the critical point where the EOS may return non-physical roots. Non-isothermal gas flow (temperature gradients along the pipe) is not yet supported — planned for a future release.

Equation of state

Density from pressure and temperature.

Ideal gas law

For a single-component ideal gas, the density at any point in the network is:

$$\rho = \frac{P M}{R T}$$

$P$ — absolute pressure (Pa)  ·  $M$ — molecular weight (kg mol−1)  ·  $R = 8.314\ \text{J\,mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ — universal gas constant  ·  $T$ — absolute temperature (K)

The local pressure used for each pipe link is the arithmetic mean of the link's inlet and outlet node pressures: $P_{\text{link}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(P_{\text{in}} + P_{\text{out}})$. This average is a natural choice for a one-dimensional element and avoids bias toward either end.

Viscosity, specific heat, and thermal conductivity are supplied as constants (or temperature-only functions). For typical gas pipeline applications the pressure dependence of transport properties is negligible compared with the pressure dependence of density.

The EquationOfState interface

Density is computed via a pluggable EquationOfState abstract class with a single method: 

class EquationOfState(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def density(self, pressure_pa: float, temperature_c: float) -> float:
        ...

The current implementation ships IdealGasEOS. Future releases will add Peng–Robinson and other cubic EOS — especially for compositional simulation via the thermo library.

Algorithm

Outer density loop wrapping SIMPLE.

The solver uses a segregated outer–inner structure. The inner loop is the standard SIMPLE hydraulic solver; the outer loop iterates on density:

  1. Snapshot current density $\rho^{\,n}$ on each link from the EOS at the current pressure field.
  2. Run the SIMPLE hydraulic solver to convergence. Within each SIMPLE iteration, $\rho(P, T)$ is re-evaluated automatically as node pressures are updated.
  3. Compute the maximum relative density change across all links: $\varepsilon = \max_i |\Delta\rho_i| / \rho_i^{\,n}$.
  4. If $\varepsilon < \varepsilon_{\text{tol}}$ → converged. Otherwise go to step 1.

The default tolerance is $\varepsilon_{\text{tol}} = 10^{-4}$. For moderate pressure ratios (up to ~ 3:1) the outer loop typically converges in fewer than 10 iterations.

Mass conservation in a compressible network

The SIMPLE solver enforces continuity at each node in terms of mass flow — $\sum \dot{m} = 0$ at each internal node. Because $\dot{m} = \rho \, Q$, the volumetric flow rates at different pressures are not equal even at a simple junction, but mass is conserved exactly (within the convergence tolerance of the hydraulic solver).

Validation

Physically consistent results.

Ideal gas density ratio

For air at standard conditions (101 325 Pa, 20 °C, $M = 0.028\,964\ \text{kg/mol}$), the solver returns $\rho = 1.204\ \text{kg/m}^3$ — matching the analytical ideal gas value within floating-point precision. Doubling the pressure exactly doubles the density, consistent with $\rho \propto P$.

Mass conservation at junctions

In a two-pipe series network at 300 kPa and 100 kPa, the mass flow through both pipes agrees to within $10^{-4}$ relative error — confirming that the SIMPLE continuity constraint is satisfied in mass terms even when volumetric flow rates differ due to density variation.

Density gradient

The inlet node density is always greater than the outlet node density when $P_{\text{in}} > P_{\text{out}}$, consistent with $\rho \propto P$ for an ideal gas at constant temperature. This is verified automatically in the test suite on every commit.
